Knight

An action RPG in Unreal Engine 5, built on the Gameplay Ability System. The game is the excuse; the point is to use GAS the way it is actually designed to be used, rather than hand-rolling combat for the fourth time because it is faster on day one.

Why GAS, deliberately

Every gameplay programmer can build a combat system. Health as a float, an attack function, a cooldown timer, and it works — until you need a buff that scales with another stat, a damage-over-time that stacks, an ability that is predicted client-side, or a designer who wants to change any of it without a programmer. Then the hand-rolled version becomes a second, worse ability framework living inside your game.

GAS is Unreal's answer to that, it is what studios building this genre actually use, and it has a reputation for being hard to learn because it is a real framework with opinions. I chose it precisely for that: I would rather spend personal-project time on the thing that is hard to pick up on the job than on the thing I can already do.

What it covers

  • Abilities as first-class objects — granted, activated, cancelled and cost-checked through the ability system, rather than as functions on a character class.
  • Attributes and effects — health, stamina and damage expressed as attributes modified by gameplay effects, so buffs, costs and damage all flow through one mechanism.
  • Tags driving state — the part that changes how you think. Conditions become tag queries rather than booleans scattered across the character.

This is in progress and the page says so on purpose. Gameplay Ability System depth was a gap I identified in myself, and closing it in public is more honest — and more useful in an interview — than only showing finished things.
