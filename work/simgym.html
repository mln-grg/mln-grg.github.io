SimGym — scenario creator

SimGym is a PC VR training simulator. Every new training scenario used to be an engineering task: someone described what they wanted, it went in a backlog, and weeks later they got something close. I built the mode that removed us from that loop.

Company
i3 Simulations
Engine
Unreal Engine, PC VR
Core system
Multigrid authoring
Users
Instructors, not engineers

The problem

A training simulator lives or dies on the breadth of its content. One scenario is a demo; forty scenarios is a product. But scenario content was authored in-engine by programmers, which meant the people who actually understood the training — instructors and subject matter experts — could only describe what they wanted and wait. Every iteration cost a round trip through engineering, and the total content the product could hold was capped by how much programmer time it could buy.

What I built

A creator mode inside the application, built on a multigrid system: a structured authoring space where scenario elements are placed, constrained and related to one another, rather than positioned freehand and hoped over. The grid is what makes the tool usable by non-engineers — it turns "arrange this correctly" from a judgement call into a set of legal moves.

The design constraint that shaped everything: whatever an instructor builds must be a valid scenario. A tool that lets its user produce something broken and only tells them at runtime hasn't removed the engineer from the loop, it has just moved when they get called. So validity is enforced during authoring, not after it.

Why it mattered

  • It changed who could make content. Instructors author their own detailed simulations. Engineering stopped being the bottleneck on the product's most valuable axis.
  • It changed iteration speed. Adjusting a scenario went from a ticket to a session, which means scenarios get refined rather than shipped as first drafts.
  • It is the same instinct as my UI framework work. Given a choice between building a feature and building the thing that lets other people build features, I have repeatedly chosen the second — and on both projects it was the decision that aged best.
