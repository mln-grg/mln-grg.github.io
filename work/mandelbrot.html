Mandelbrot

A Mandelbrot set viewer in C++20 with SDL2. Small on purpose: one file, one loop, and two problems worth solving properly. Making the colour continuous instead of banded, and making the zoom hold the point you are pointing at.

Language
C++20, SDL2
Render
500 x 500, 500 iterations
Threads
One
Source
github.com/mln-grg/mandelbrot

The banding problem

The obvious way to colour the set is by iteration count: how many steps before the point escapes. It works, and it looks wrong. Iteration count is an integer, so the image comes out in hard rings, and every zoom makes the rings more obvious rather than less.

The fix is to stop bailing out early. With a large escape radius, 256 here rather than the usual 2, the magnitude at the moment of escape carries information about where inside that step the point actually left. The fractional escape time is then

i + 1 - log2(log2(|z|))

which is continuous across the boundary between one iteration count and the next. Normalise that, use it to interpolate through a fifteen stop palette, and the gradient stays smooth at any depth.

Zooming without losing your place

A viewer that zooms to the centre of the window is a viewer you fight. Every time you get close to something interesting it slides off the edge and you have to pan back. So the wheel handler converts the cursor position to a complex coordinate first, applies the zoom factor to the view size, then moves the centre so that same coordinate lands back under the cursor.

That is four lines of arithmetic and it is the difference between exploring the set and chasing it. It is also why the GIF above works: the cursor was parked on one point in seahorse valley and never moved for forty four notches.

What it does not do

  • 500 iterations, fixed. Deep zooms into the boundary run out of detail before they run out of precision, which is what the flat regions at the end of a long zoom are.
  • Double precision gives out somewhere around a view width of 1e-14. Past that you need perturbation theory and arbitrary precision reference orbits, which is a different project.
  • Single threaded, and the row loop is embarrassingly parallel. Splitting it is the obvious first change and a fragment shader is the obvious second one, which is the same ladder automata-engine is climbing.
